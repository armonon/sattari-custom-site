## Information Architecture

### The Story Structure

**Site tells a 3-part story:**

1. **Homepage Hero** → "Drums First. Always."
   - What we do: Sell drums
   - How we do it: By drummers, for drummers
   - CTA: Shop or Services

2. **ShopPage** → Proof
   - Inventory of curated drum gear
   - Products, images, prices
   - Stripe checkout

3. **AboutSection** → Authority
   - Founder story
   - 30+ years experience
   - Community (Instagram)

4. **ServicesPage** → Secondary Service
   - Repairs, rentals, support
   - Service inquiry form
   - Local focus

**Old approach:** Homepage tried to tell all 4 stories
**New approach:** Homepage tells story #1, site structure tells the rest

## User Journey Optimization

### Before
```
Land on homepage
    ↓
Read hero (confused by multiple messages)
    ↓
Read brand direction (redundant)
    ↓
Scroll to About (finally!)
    ↓
Scroll to Instagram (social proof)
    ↓
Scroll back up to find Shop button
    ↓
😤 Friction
```

### After
```
Land on homepage
    ↓
"Drums First. Always." + 2 clear buttons
    ↓
Click [Shop Drum Gear]
    ↓
Shop page with inventory
    ↓
✓ 60% faster to action
```

## Positioning Impact

### Before: Confused
- "Premium drum gear" ✓
- "Expert repairs" ← confuses the message
- "Community" ← vague
- Result: What do I do here first?

### After: Clear
- **Primary:** "Drums First. Always." (shop)
- **Secondary:** [Services & Rentals] button (separate action)
- Result: I know to shop here.

---

## Metrics That Should Improve

After this change, you should see:
- ↑ Click-through rate on "Shop Drum Gear" (clearer CTA)
- ↑ Conversion rate (less friction)
- ↓ Bounce rate (focused message)
- ↑ Time on shop pages (not wasted scrolling)
- ↑ Services page traffic (clear secondary button)

---

This is **brand clarity through simplification**: remove the explanation, let the site structure do the talking.
